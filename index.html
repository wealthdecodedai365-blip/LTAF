Authenticated is not authorised. Securing API-driven loan disbursements with the Lending Transaction Authorisation Framework (LTAF), by Kesava Rao Sanapathi.

01

The controls existed. They were checked in the wrong place.

The attackers did not break bank security. They became the lender. From compromised lender servers, they called disbursement APIs with valid partner-bank credentials and pushed payouts for loans that did not exist.

Lenders already eSign loan documents, penny-drop bank accounts and approve disbursements in their loan systems. Every one of those checks is verified inside the lender's own systems, which is exactly where the attacker sat.

API authentication tells the bank who is calling. A loan disbursement also needs proof that this payment is an approved disbursement: this loan, this amount, this account, once.

02 · The idea · Lending Transaction Authorisation Framework (LTAF)

Every loan disbursement is verified outside the lender's systems, against the approved disbursement record and the beneficiary's bank. An independent Registry issues a single-use Disbursement Authorisation Token (DAT), and the bank releases funds only after verifying it.

Zero-touch by design. The borrower journey stays the same and there is no manual approval queue.

What changes

Every genuine loan disbursement is preceded by a record: a sanctioned loan, an approved amount and tranche, and a verified destination account. LTAF moves the verification of that record out of the lender's systems and makes the disbursing bank enforce it.

Four trust domains. Assume the lender can be compromised.

Verification, signing and the audit trail sit where the attacker cannot reach. The lender carries the evidence but is never trusted to vouch for it.

Bound to the exact payment

Loan, tranche, amount, currency and account. Change any field and the bank rejects the payment.

Short-lived and single use

A unique ID is consumed atomically at the bank, so replays and concurrent attempts fail.

Evidence-backed

The token references the approval and signed documents the Registry verified, never raw borrower data.

Pulled by the bank

The lender never handles the token, which removes the delivery channel as an attack surface.

07

Five guardrails that close the side doors.

Disbursement-only account

No debit without a valid token. Other outflows use a separate account.

Every channel

API, internet banking, bulk upload and branch all require the token.

Fail closed

If verification is unavailable or unclear, funds are held.

Circuit breakers

Lender baselines hold abnormal spikes. Normal campaigns still flow.

Permitted payees only

Borrower's own account by default. Dealers and builders from a verified payee master.

What LTAF does not do

  • Prevent the initial intrusion. Patching, credential hygiene and endpoint protection still matter.
  • Stop a genuine borrower colluding with an insider. That is credit fraud.
  • Remove risk from the Registry itself, which must be engineered and assured as a high-value target.

Open questions

  • Who should run the Registry: the disbursing bank, a specialist provider or a shared utility?
  • Will an automated, independent check meet dual-approval expectations in RBI's 2026 NBFC cybersecurity directions?
  • What latency and cost per disbursement are acceptable at scale?

Data protection by design

Aligned with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025. LTAF uses the data lenders already hold for disbursal, for a closely related purpose, with less of it.

Lender stays the data fiduciary

The Registry acts as its data processor under a contract covering purpose, security, breach notice, audit and deletion.

Consent notice

Existing loan consent states that disbursement details are verified by an independent authorisation service. One line, no new step.

Minimum data

The Registry holds hashes and tokens, not raw account numbers or borrower details.

No Aadhaar numbers

eSign signatures are verified, but Aadhaar numbers are never stored.

Retention and use

Kept only as long as RBI record rules require, then deleted. Never reused for marketing or scoring.

Hosted in India

Registry data stays in India, in line with RBI expectations for payment-related data.

Indicative view, not legal advice. Confirm with data-protection counsel.

Lending went digital on the speed of APIs. Its security now has to prove, independently, that every rupee leaving a disbursement account was meant to.

Next step: a shadow-mode pilot with one lender, one disbursing bank and one product. Issue and check tokens without enforcing them, then switch on enforcement once zero added borrower steps and red-team results are proven.
